## 2 months to 5 years: what a CDC milestone means

The CDC pages say: "Developmental milestones are things most children (75% or more) can do by a certain age."
So a CDC milestone at an age is a skill that most children already have by that age. About 1 child in 4 may not have it yet, and that alone is not a diagnosis.
The CDC pages say: "Learn the Signs. Act Early. materials are not a substitute for standardized, validated developmental screening tools."

## 2 months to 5 years: how the 2022 checklists were made

In 2022 the CDC and the American Academy of Pediatrics revised the checklists. An expert group set 11 rules for them.
One rule: use milestones that most children (75% or more) are expected to do by the age of each health check. Another rule: use milestones that are easy to see in daily life.
The revision added checklists for 15 and 30 months. Many old milestones were removed or replaced. Of the milestones that moved to a new age, about two in three moved to an older age.
About 80% of the final milestones had normative data from at least one source. Social-emotional and cognitive milestones had the least data.

## 2 months to 5 years: when to act

The CDC says: "You know your child best. Don't wait. If your child is not meeting one or more milestones, has lost skills he or she once had, or you have other concerns, act early. Talk with your child's doctor, share your concerns, and ask about developmental screening."

## Strength of the evidence

Frecklebubby note: if 75% or more of children can do a skill by the CDC age, the median age when children first do it is the same or earlier. So a CDC age is not the age when a typical child first does the skill. Other sources, such as the WHO motor windows, give a median or a range, so their ages differ from CDC ages.
