## 4 to 18 months: what the WHO windows are

WHO studied 816 healthy children in Ghana, India, Norway, Oman and the USA. Trained fieldworkers checked six gross motor milestones from 4 to 24 months of age.
Each window goes from the 1st to the 99th percentile of the age when the children first did the milestone.
WHO says the windows show normal variation in the age of achievement among healthy children.
WHO says the windows can signal the need for proper screening when a child seems late. A window is not a diagnosis.
WHO draws the windows with no marks inside them. The median (50th percentile) below comes from the WHO percentile table.
WHO gives months as days divided by 30.4375.

## 4 to 9 months: sitting without support

WHO test: the child sits up straight with the head erect for at least 10 seconds. The child does not use arms or hands to balance or to support the body.
WHO window for sitting without support: 3.8 to 9.2 months (115 to 279 days). Median 5.9 months (179 days).

## 5 to 12 months: standing with assistance

WHO test: the child stands upright on both feet and holds a stable object, such as furniture, with both hands. The child does not lean on it. The legs hold most of the body weight for at least 10 seconds.
WHO window for standing with assistance: 4.8 to 11.4 months (147 to 348 days). Median 7.4 months (226 days).

## 5 to 14 months: hands-and-knees crawling

WHO test: the child moves forward or backward on hands and knees, one side then the other. The stomach does not touch the floor. The child makes at least three movements in a row.
WHO window for hands-and-knees crawling: 5.2 to 13.5 months (157 to 409 days). Median 8.3 months (254 days).
In the WHO study, 35 of 816 children (4.3%) never crawled on hands and knees. WHO left them out of the crawling numbers.

## 6 to 14 months: walking with assistance

WHO test: the child is upright with the back straight. The child holds a stable object, such as furniture, with one or both hands and takes at least five steps, sideways or forward.
WHO window for walking with assistance: 5.9 to 13.7 months (181 to 418 days). Median 9.0 months (275 days).
The WHO windows table gives the start of this window as 6.0 months. The percentile table and the paper give 5.9 months.

## 7 to 17 months: standing alone

WHO test: the child stands upright on both feet, not on the toes, with the back straight. The legs hold all of the weight. The child does not touch a person or an object for at least 10 seconds.
WHO window for standing alone: 6.9 to 16.9 months (211 to 514 days). Median 10.8 months (330 days).

## 8 to 18 months: walking alone

WHO test: the child takes at least five steps alone, upright, with the back straight. The child does not touch a person or an object.
WHO window for walking alone: 8.2 to 17.6 months (250 to 534 days). Median 12.0 months (365 days).

## 4 to 18 months: order of the milestones

In about 90% of the WHO children, five milestones came in a fixed order: sitting without support, standing with assistance, walking with assistance, standing alone, walking alone.
Hands-and-knees crawling came at different points among the earlier milestones.

## Strength of the evidence

This is a large study of healthy children, with the same trained checks in five countries. The children were breastfed and their mothers did not smoke. The study chose children from well-off families with good conditions for growth.
Assessments were monthly in year 1 and every 2 months in year 2. In Ghana and Norway the motor checks started later for some children, at 6 to 14 months of age.
The CDC checklist uses a different rule: a skill that 75% or more of children can do by an age. The WHO windows and the CDC ages answer different questions.
