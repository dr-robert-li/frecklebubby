## 7 to 12 months: crawling and moving (Baby moves)

Best Start in Life says babies will probably start crawling at around 7 to 10 months. It says not all babies crawl, and some shuffle on their bottoms.
If you are worried that your baby shows no signs of moving by 12 months, it says to ask your health visitor for advice.
It says you can start tummy time from birth with the baby on your chest, but only when you are wide awake. Build up the time day by day, then try the floor.
Page: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/baby/baby-moves/

## Newborn to 5 years: sleep changes (Your baby's sleep patterns)

Best Start in Life says some newborns sleep about 8 hours a day in total and others up to about 18 hours, and both are normal. A newborn is very unlikely to sleep through the night.
At 3 to 6 months it says babies need fewer night feeds, and some sleep 8 hours or longer at night, but not all.
From around 6 months it says babies may sleep up to 12 hours at night, but teething and hunger can wake them.
From 12 to 24 months it says most babies sleep about 12 to 15 hours in total.
At 2 to 3 years it says about 12 to 14 hours in total, naps included. At 3 to 5 years it says most children need about 12 hours, and some still nap.
Page: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/baby/baby-basics/newborn-and-baby-sleeping-advice-for-parents/your-babys-sleep-patterns/

## 18 months: tantrums and feelings (Help your toddler with their emotions)

Best Start in Life says that at around 18 months a toddler may have more tantrums and show more frustration.
Page: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/toddler/help-your-toddler-with-their-emotions/

## Under 2 years and 2 to 5 years: screen time

For babies and toddlers under 2, Best Start in Life says to avoid screen time. If you use screens, use them for shared activities with your child.
For children aged 2 to 5, it says to limit screen time and not to use screens for more than 1 hour a day. It suggests content with simple stories and clear, slow speech.
Pages: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/baby/baby-moves/ and https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/toddler/activities-for-toddlers/

## 0 to 5 years: talking, and when to ask for help

Best Start in Life says children learn to talk at different ages. If you are worried, speak to your health visitor or nursery key worker, or contact your local speech and language therapy service.
It says the health visitor talks with you about your baby's development at about 12 months, and offers a review at about 2 to 2 and a half years.
It says a child may have no routine development review between 1 and 2 years. A health visitor can still help if you think your child needs extra support.
It says that from 3 to 5 years children start to use longer sentences, such as "I want a drink now".
Pages: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/baby/learning-to-talk/listening-and-learning-6-to-12-months/ and https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/toddler/learning-to-talk/first-words-and-little-sentences-1-to-2-years/

## Newborn: dummies and breastfeeding (Understanding your baby)

Best Start in Life says a dummy can soothe a baby. For a breastfed baby it says a dummy can be given after 4 weeks. For a formula-fed baby it says at any time.
In its 6 to 12 months advice, the NHS talk page says to offer a dummy only at sleep time and to aim to stop dummies by 12 months (see the file nhs-help-baby-learn-to-talk).
Page: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/baby/baby-basics/bonding-with-your-baby/understanding-your-baby/
