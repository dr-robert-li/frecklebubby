## 6 months to 3 years: separation anxiety and fear of strangers

Babies and toddlers often get clingy and cry if you or their other carers leave them, even for a short time.
Separation anxiety and fear of strangers is common in young children between the ages of 6 months and 3 years, although it can last longer and may also happen at other times of stress or anxiety.
But it's a normal part of your child's development and they usually grow out of it.

## 6 months to 3 years: why separation anxiety happens

But separation anxiety is a sign your baby now realises they're a separate person from you and how dependent they are on the people who care for them.
That can include their grandparents or professionals closely involved with their care, as well as their parents.
As they get more aware of their surroundings, your baby's strong relationship with this small group means they do not feel so safe without you.
Their growing awareness of the world around them can also make them feel unsafe or upset in new situations or with new people, even if you are there.
In fact, separation anxiety is usually a sign of how well you have bonded with them.

## 6 months to 3 years: how to help with separation anxiety

Playing games of peek-a-boo are a good way to show your baby you always come back. If you need to leave the room, hearing your voice can reassure them you have not gone even if they cannot see you.
When your baby can move, letting them follow you if it's safe to do so can help them understand you have not disappeared entirely when you pop out of the room.
You could start by leaving them in someone else's care for a few minutes while you nip to the local shop. Leave your baby with someone they know well so they still feel comfortable and safe in your absence.
Gradually work towards longer separations, and then leaving them in less familiar settings.
Talk to your toddler about what you're going to do when you see them again so they have something to look forward to with you.
It may comfort your baby to have something they identify with you – like a scarf with your scent on or a favourite toy – close by. This may reassure them while you are away.
When you leave your baby, however sad or worried you may be feeling, smile and wave goodbye confidently and happily, otherwise they will pick up on your tension.

## 6 months to 3 years: when to get help for separation anxiety

It's completely natural for babies and toddlers to cry when they part from their main caregiver. But as babies get older, they're more able to understand that people and things exist even when they cannot see them.
If your child's separation anxiety is causing them a lot of distress, they are upset for a long time after you have left them, or it has been going on for more than a few weeks, talk to your health visitor.
