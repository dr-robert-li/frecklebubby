## Birth to 12 months: newborn reflexes

A reflex is a movement that the baby does not choose to make. Many movements in the first weeks are reflexes.
The AAP says not all babies get and lose these reflexes at exactly the same time. The ages below are a general guide.
All six reflexes in the AAP table are present from birth.

## Birth to 2 months: stepping reflex

Hold the baby under the arms, with the head supported, and let the soles touch a flat surface. The baby puts one foot in front of the other.
The AAP gives 2 months as the age when the stepping reflex goes away. Walking comes back toward the end of the first year as a learned skill.

## Birth to 2 months: Moro (startle) reflex

When the head moves suddenly or the baby hears a loud noise, the baby stretches out the arms, legs and neck and then quickly brings the arms together. The baby may cry.
The AAP says the Moro reflex usually peaks in the first month and starts to go away after about 2 months. The AAP table gives 2 months.

## Birth to 4 months: rooting reflex

When you stroke the cheek or mouth, the baby turns the head toward your hand. This helps the baby find the nipple.
The AAP gives 4 months as the age when the rooting reflex goes away.

## Birth to 6 months: palmar grasp reflex

When you stroke the palm, the baby grips your finger. In the first days the grip is very strong, but the baby can let go suddenly. The AAP says do not test whether the baby can hold their own weight.
The AAP gives 5 to 6 months as the age when the palmar grasp reflex goes away.

## Birth to 7 months: tonic neck (fencing) reflex

When the head turns to one side, the arm on that side straightens and the other arm bends.
The AAP gives 5 to 7 months as the age when the tonic neck reflex goes away.

## Birth to 12 months: plantar grasp reflex

When you stroke the sole of the foot, the toes curl tightly.
The AAP gives 9 to 12 months as the age when the plantar grasp reflex goes away.

## Any age: when to tell the doctor about reflexes

The AAP says the Moro and tonic neck reflexes should be the same on both sides of the body. Tell your baby's doctor if a reflex seems different on one side, or if the baby moves one side of the body better than the other.
