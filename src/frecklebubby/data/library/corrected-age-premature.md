## Birth to 2 years: what the sources say about corrected age

The NHS says: "If your baby was born prematurely, their developmental age will be calculated from your original due date, not from the actual date they were born, until they are 2 years old."
The AAP says to use the corrected age (also called adjusted age) of a premature baby to track development until 2 years of age.
Both sources give the same rule: count from the due date, not the birth date, until 2 years.

## Birth to 2 years: how to work out corrected age

The AAP method: take the baby's age in weeks since birth. Subtract the number of weeks the baby was born early. The result is the corrected age.
The AAP finds the weeks born early as 40 minus the weeks of pregnancy at birth.
AAP example: a baby born at 32 weeks was 8 weeks early. At 16 weeks after birth (4 months), the corrected age is 2 months. Expect the skills of a 2-month-old baby born at term.
The AAP preemie page counts a month as 4 weeks.

## 2 years: catching up

The AAP says that by 2 years of age most children have caught up to the typical milestone range. If a child has not caught up, the child may need extra support for a longer time.
The AAP says to ask your baby's doctor about early intervention.

## Note on the sources

One AAP page says that a pregnancy is now considered full term at 39 weeks, but it still counts the weeks born early from 40. Counting from the due date, as the NHS says, gives the same result.
The AAP preemie page is for babies born more than 3 weeks early.
