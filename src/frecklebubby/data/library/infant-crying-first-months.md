## 0 to 12 weeks: how much babies cry

A 2017 review put together 28 diary studies with 8,690 babies.
In the first 6 weeks, the average fussing and crying time was about 2 hours a day (117 to 133 minutes). Babies vary a lot around this average.
By 10 to 12 weeks, the average dropped to about 1 hour a day (68 minutes).
The review says fussing and crying time drops a lot after 8 to 9 weeks of age.
The review found no clear evidence for one crying peak at 6 weeks in all studies.

## 0 to 12 weeks: colic

The review used the "modified Wessel criteria" to define colic. The NHS says a baby may have colic if they cry more than 3 hours a day, 3 days a week, for at least 1 week, but are otherwise healthy (see the file nhs-colic).
Colic was found in 17% to 25% of babies in the first 6 weeks, in 11% at 8 to 9 weeks, and in 0.6% at 10 to 12 weeks. The review says colic is rare after 9 weeks.
The NHS says colic can start when a baby is a few weeks old and usually stops by 3 to 4 months (see the file nhs-colic).

## 0 to 3 months: what the CDC says

The CDC 2-month tips say: "Infant crying is often worse in the first few months of life, but it gets better!"
The CDC says never shake a baby. If you get upset when your baby cries, put the baby in a safe place and walk away. Check on the baby every 5 to 10 minutes.

## Strength of the evidence

This is a systematic review and meta-analysis of diary studies. Colic was least common in the studies from Denmark and Japan. The numbers are averages. They are not a limit for a single baby.
