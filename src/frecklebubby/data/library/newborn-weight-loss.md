## First 3 weeks: weight loss and regain after birth

NICE (the UK guideline body) says it is common for babies to lose some weight in the first days of life. It says this is normal and usually comes from changes in body fluid.
NICE says this weight loss usually stops after about 3 or 4 days of life.
NICE says most babies are back to their birth weight by 3 weeks of age.
The NHS gives the same 3-week point (see the file nhs-baby-weight-height).

## First 3 weeks: when health professionals look more closely

NICE tells health professionals to assess the baby and to give feeding support if a baby loses more than 10% of birth weight in the early days.
NICE also tells them to consider more action if the baby is not back to birth weight by 3 weeks.
These are rules for health professionals, not for parents to apply at home. The NHS says a midwife or health visitor will support you if your baby loses a large amount of weight or is not back to birth weight by 3 weeks. If you are worried about your baby's weight or feeding, talk to your midwife, health visitor or GP.

## After 3 weeks: weight gain

The NHS says babies usually gain weight fastest in the first 6 months, and that growth then slows as they become toddlers (see the file nhs-baby-weight-height).
Frecklebubby found no NHS, CDC or WHO parent page that gives the age of "growth spurts". The NHS says growth spurts, teething and illness can affect sleep, and a baby may cluster feed for a few days during a growth spurt.

## Strength of the evidence

NICE NG75 is UK clinical guidance written for health professionals. The 10% and 3-week points are points for a closer look, not a diagnosis.
