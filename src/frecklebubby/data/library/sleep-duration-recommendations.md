## AASM 2016 recommended sleep by age

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) consensus panel recommends these amounts of sleep per 24 hours, on a regular basis, to promote optimal health:
- Infants 4 to 12 months: 12 to 16 hours, including naps.
- Children 1 to 2 years: 11 to 14 hours, including naps.
- Children 3 to 5 years: 10 to 13 hours, including naps.
- Children 6 to 12 years: 9 to 12 hours.
- Teenagers 13 to 18 years: 8 to 10 hours.
The AASM did not give a recommendation for infants younger than 4 months because normal sleep amount and patterns vary so widely at that age.
Citation: Paruthi S, et al. Recommended amount of sleep for pediatric populations: a consensus statement of the American Academy of Sleep Medicine. J Clin Sleep Med 2016;12(6):785-786. doi:10.5664/jcsm.5866.

## National Sleep Foundation 2015 recommended sleep by age

The National Sleep Foundation (NSF) expert panel found these sleep durations appropriate for healthy people with normal sleep:
- Newborns, 0 to 3 months: 14 to 17 hours.
- Infants, 4 to 11 months: 12 to 15 hours.
- Toddlers, 1 to 2 years: 11 to 14 hours.
- Preschoolers, 3 to 5 years: 10 to 13 hours.
- School-aged children: 9 to 11 hours.
The NSF page also says an hour or two on either side of a range may be appropriate depending on the person. The newborn figure includes daytime naps.
Citation: Hirshkowitz M, et al. National Sleep Foundation's sleep time duration recommendations: methodology and results summary. Sleep Health 2015;1(1):40-43. doi:10.1016/j.sleh.2014.12.010.

## How the two sets differ

For infants the AASM range (12 to 16 hours, 4 to 12 months) is a little wider and higher than the NSF range (12 to 15 hours, 4 to 11 months). For toddlers and preschoolers the ranges are the same (11 to 14 and 10 to 13 hours). Only the NSF gives a figure for newborns under 4 months. Both are for healthy children and are guides, not targets: a baby who sleeps outside the range is not necessarily unwell, and parents who are worried about too little or too much sleep should ask their health professional.
