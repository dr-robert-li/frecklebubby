## What the sources say

The Lullaby Trust (UK charity) gives no number of hours. It says a baby may fall asleep in a car seat while you drive. It says to take the baby out as soon as you arrive and to place the baby on a firm, flat surface to sleep.
The Lullaby Trust says car seats are for transport only. They are not made for long sleeps.
The Lullaby Trust says to avoid driving long distances with pre-term and young babies. It says to stop and take regular breaks on a long drive.
The Lullaby Trust says to stop at once if a baby slumps forward. Take the baby out of the seat and sit the baby upright.
The Lullaby Trust links the advice to a 2017 University of Bristol study. It found that pre-term and young babies may have breathing problems in an upright car seat for a long time.
The American Academy of Pediatrics (HealthyChildren.org) says to take a break every 2 to 3 hours on a day trip. On a night trip it says every 4 to 6 hours. It says car seats are not ideal for very extended periods when the baby is not closely watched.

## Strength of the evidence

The study is a small pilot. It tested 19 term and 21 preterm babies, about 2 weeks old on average (range 1 to 65 days), in a simulator for a short time. Oxygen levels dropped and heart and breathing rates rose in the seat at 40 degrees, more so with vibration. The authors say a larger study is needed.
Frecklebubby found no official source that sets a 2-hour limit for car seat use. Many parent websites and some hospital leaflets quote "2 hours". We could not check those hospital leaflets. Treat "2 hours" as a common rule of thumb, not a tested limit.
The sources do not give an age range for the advice. They refer to young babies and pre-term babies. The study tested babies up to 65 days old.
